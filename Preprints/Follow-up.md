## 1. The Nuremberg Promise vs. Contemporary Enforcement Decay

The founding premise of modern international criminal and human rights law—crystallized at **Nuremberg (1945–1946)** and codified in the **1950 Nuremberg Principles**—was a radical rejection of state positivism as a shield for atrocity. Nuremberg established two non-negotiable axioms:
1. **Primacy of Peremptory Norms (*Jus Cogens*):** Certain prohibitions—against torture, crimes against humanity, war crimes, enforced disappearance, and systematic persecution—bind all states universally and cannot be derogated from by domestic statute, administrative decree, or treaty reservation (later codified in Articles 53 and 64 of the **1969 Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties**).
2. **Piercing the Veil of Domestic Legality:** Under Nuremberg Principle II, the fact that internal domestic law does not impose a penalty for an act which constitutes a crime under international law does not relieve the person who committed the act from responsibility.

Yet in the decades since Nuremberg, enforcement has progressively hollowed out that peremptory core. Rather than openly repudiating human rights treaties, states have perfected **procedural nullification**:
* **Admissibility Labyrinths & Multi-Year Backlogs:** Individual petitions before UN treaty bodies and regional courts routinely languish for half a decade or longer on preliminary exhaustion and technical admissibility objections, turning time itself into an instrument of impunity.
* **Non-Enforcement of Views and Judgments:** Even when an HRD or survivor survives years of procedural attrition to win a favorable Decision or Concluding Observation, states routinely treat UN treaty body *Views* as non-binding "recommendations," leaving victims without compensation, medical/psychological rehabilitation, or structural reform.
* **Weaponized Extraterritoriality:** Simultaneously, states that invoke strict territorial sovereignty to block international human rights scrutiny freely project power across borders through **transnational repression**—abusing Interpol Red Notices, revoking passports, and conducting extraterritorial surveillance while hiding behind diplomatic immunity.

## 2. Opaque Jurisprudence and the Weaponization of Dualism Against Rehabilitation and Transitional Justice

At the heart of this regression is the transformation of **legal dualism** from a technical doctrine of legislative incorporation into an **all-purpose escape hatch from *jus cogens* obligations**.

In classical international law theory, **dualism** holds that international treaties and domestic law operate on distinct planes until parliament enacts incorporating legislation. In a good-faith constitutional order, dualism is accompanied by a strong interpretive presumption that domestic statutes must be construed in harmony with international human rights obligations—and certainly never in violation of *jus cogens* prohibitions.

In contemporary practice, however, **opaque, formalistic jurisprudence** has inverted that presumption:

| Legal & Moral Dimension | The Nuremberg / *Jus Cogens* Standard | Contemporary Dualist & Formalist Evasion | Impact on Survivors & Transitional Justice |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Status of Peremptory Norms (*Jus Cogens*)** | Non-derogable, supreme over conflicting domestic rules; imposes *erga omnes* duties to investigate, prosecute, and repair. | Treated as abstract, non-self-executing ideals unenforceable in domestic courts absent bespoke statutory incorporation. | Survivors of torture or state violence are told their rights exist "on the international plane" while domestic courts refuse jurisdiction or immunity waivers. |
| **Role of Jurisprudence** | Clear, principled reasoning grounded in human dignity (*UDHR* Preamble) and individual moral accountability. | Dense, self-referential proceduralism ("margin of appreciation," sovereign immunity, state-secrets privilege) obscuring substantive violations. | Jurisprudence becomes unintelligible to the victims it purports to protect, creating secondary **evaluative and cognitive harm**. |
| **Right to Rehabilitation & Reparation** | Integral to justice (UNCAT Art. 14; UN Basic Principles on the Right to a Remedy and Reparation, 2005): holistic medical, psychological, legal, and social restoration. | Reduced to discretionary administrative welfare or blocked entirely behind sovereign immunity and limitation statutes. | Perpetuates ongoing trauma; states evade structural guarantees of non-recurrence, aborting genuine **transitional justice**. |
| **Protection of Human Rights Defenders** | **UN Declaration on Human Rights Defenders (A/RES/53/144, 1998):** Affirmative state duty to protect HRDs, ensure access to international bodies (Art. 9(4)), and refrain from retaliation. | Criminalization of documentation, administrative harassment, surveillance, and dismissal of HRD submissions as "uncorroborated." | Isolates defenders from institutional support, forcing them to operate without state protection or reliable legal remedies. |

When jurisprudence detaches from *jus cogens* norms, law ceases to be an instrument of moral reckoning and becomes a **self-sealing administrative buffer**—one that acknowledges human rights rhetorically in Geneva or Strasbourg while permitting total impunity in Dublin, Port of Spain, or any domestic ministry that simply declines to incorporate a treaty into national statute.

## 3. The Loss of Moral Grounding: Abandoning the UDHR and the UN Declaration on Human Rights Defenders

This procedural drift represents a direct abandonment of the twin moral charters of modern human rights protection:

1. **The 1948 Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR):** Born directly out of the ashes of World War II and the Nuremberg revelations, the UDHR opens with an explicit warning: *"disregard and contempt for human rights have resulted in barbarous acts which have outraged the conscience of mankind"*—and insists that human rights must be protected by the rule of law so that human beings are not compelled to have recourse, as a last resort, to rebellion against tyranny and oppression. When international jurisprudence buries gross violations beneath impenetrable dualist technicalities, it severs law from **"the conscience of mankind."**
2. **The 1998 UN Declaration on Human Rights Defenders (A/RES/53/144):** Adopted fifty years after the UDHR, the Declaration affirms that every person has the right, individually and in association with others, to promote and to strive for the protection and realization of human rights (Art. 1), including the right to unhindered access to and communication with international bodies (Art. 9(4)) and the right to effective protection against retaliation (Art. 12). In reality, defenders documenting state violence or transnational repression face systematic abandonment: international mechanisms lack enforcement teeth, domestic courts invoke dualism to ignore treaty-body findings, and defenders are left bearing the psychological, financial, and cognitive cost of resistance alone.
